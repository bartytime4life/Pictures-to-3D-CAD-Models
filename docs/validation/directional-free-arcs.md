## A derived directional correction beyond the leading term

For unit radial direction `n` and perpendicular `t`, define `a=n.T S n`, `b=t.T S t`, `c=n.T S t`, `D=det(S)` and `v=D/a`. An isolated disk's local half-level displacement has the formal large-radius expansion

```text
d(R,n) = v/(2R) + v² [3/8 - b/(6a) + 17c²/(12a²)] / R³ + O(R^-5).
```

This coefficient was derived for this investigation and independently checked algebraically. In local normal/tangent coordinates the disk boundary has normal height `h(Y)=d-Y²/(2R)-Y⁴/(8R³)+...`. At normal coordinate zero the tangent conditional variance is `v`. Expanding the Gaussian mass in `h`, odd terms in `Y` vanish. With `q=(v-Y²)/2`, the Gaussian moments are `E[q³]=-v³` and `E[Y²q³]=-17v⁴/2`; substituting the second normal derivative of the joint density gives the displayed coefficient. For isotropic `S=sigma² I`, it reduces to the existing `5 sigma⁴/(24R³)` forward term.

The higher-order coefficient can be negative when tangent spread dominates. No universal finite-radius improvement, certified remainder, model-adequacy result or physical error bound follows from this formal expansion. Either displacement model must refit center and radius jointly when the center is unknown; its coefficients follow from the supplied covariance and direction. Applying one scalar inverse after a geometric circle fit discards that angular center/radius coupling. The exact finite-source comparison is retained rather than replaced by an easier asymptotic target.

## Finite-model derivatives and a conditional locality bound

For declared disk material and fixed covariance, differentiating its Gaussian mass with respect to `(C_x,C_y,R)` gives the boundary integral `R integral G_S(p-C-Ru) (u_x,u_y,1) dtheta`. The study uses that shape derivative for the joint fit and checks two angular orders plus independent central parameter differences. These are numerical derivative checks; they do not certify a global optimizer or physical uncertainty. The normal residual `(U-0.5)/|grad U|` is a first-order diagnostic, not an exact boundary distance.

There is a useful conditional isolation result. Suppose actual material `K` and a disk/complement hypothesis `D` agree throughout the whitened ball of radius `d` about query `p`. Their symmetric difference lies outside that ball, so

```text
abs((G_S * 1_K)(p) - (G_S * 1_D)(p)) <= exp(-d²/2).
```

This follows by bounding the difference integral by the two-dimensional standard-Gaussian radial tail. It bounds only probability discrepancy under the declared stationary covariance and entire-material agreement assumption. An observed circular span does not establish that agreement, and the bound does not directly bound a fitted radius: small gradients and a weak center/radius direction can amplify discrepancy. A source-aware whole-boundary/isolation test remains necessary before selecting an isolated-disk model. Unknown radiometry and piecewise thickness frames need their separate models.

## Numerical stopping is separate from model adequacy

For one radius parameter, let `g=J.T f` and `H=J.T J>0`. Before damping, the projected Gauss–Newton mapping is `r-max(0,r-g/H)`. In exact arithmetic its zero is the first-order constrained stationarity condition: `g=0` at an interior radius or `g>=0` at radius zero. The implementation uses finite-difference numerical derivatives and its existing parameter-resolution criterion, rather than an exact optimality certificate.

An objective-descent check is needed for a substantive trial step. It must not force an extra uphill trial after the current iterate already meets that original criterion. Conversely, a large proposal shortened by backtracking is not evidence of stationarity. Unsupported arithmetic, nonpositive derivative power and exhausted work remain unresolved; non-finite gradients/proposals must be rejected before capping or projection can hide them. Quadrature order agreement is not a certified residual-error bound and does not justify inventing a larger objective allowance. Numerical convergence still says nothing by itself about the physical adequacy of the Gaussian corner or its supplied covariance.

## Why short support weakens a radius estimate

For an ideal circle sampled uniformly over angles `[-phi,phi]`, the local geometric-distance Jacobian has rows `(cos(theta), sin(theta), 1)`, up to irrelevant signs. In the continuous angular limit,

```text
E[J.T J] = [[A,0,M], [0,1-A,0], [M,0,1]],
A = 1/2 + sin(2 phi)/(4 phi),    M = sin(phi)/phi.
```

The small center/radius eigenvalue behaves as `phi⁴/90`, and the Jacobian condition number as `sqrt(180)/phi²`. For an independent equal-variance radial point-error model, the radius-noise multiplier relative to a known-center estimate is `sqrt(A/(A-M²))`; it is about 5.56 at 120 degrees, 23.85 at 60 degrees and 97.24 at 30 degrees. A separate 33-point discrete SVD gives condition numbers about 10.51, 44.91 and 183.22 at those respective sweeps. These are own geometric calculations, not a calibrated noise model for interpolated photograph edges. A small residual cannot establish radius precision or rule out systematic blur/threshold/material mismatch.

The finite-disk Jacobian in the actual comparison has condition numbers about 15.23/63.12/292.56/1241.07 at 120/60/30/15 degrees. At 15 degrees, a parameter alternative 0.1 px away changes the radius by about 0.06973 px but changes the sampled level by at most `8.44e-5`, with a first-order normal-change diagnostic at most 0.000240 px. These retained alternatives demonstrate practical sensitivity despite noiseless local recovery. Under unknown isotropic blur, one observed circular half-level radius also admits a continuum of true radius/blur pairs; a rank-three local fit with fixed supplied covariance does not calibrate that covariance.

[Al-Sharadqah and Chernov](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0421) analyze circle fitting under point-coordinate error assumptions; their geometric-fit likelihood is not a forward model of Gaussian-blurred image formation. [Kornilov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10557) additionally uses measured normalized gradients under a specified likelihood. Blurred directional gradients cannot be assumed to point to the true disk center. [Chojnacki and Szpak](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06814) explicitly model PSF, discretization, quantization and noise rather than treating extracted points as unbiased. These sources motivate checking observation assumptions; none qualifies the workbench's covariance, response, point errors or manufactured dimensions.
